## Chapter 1

#### 2. Set

::: definition:Open set
Let $X$ be a topological space. The open sets in $X$ are the members of the topology.
:::

::: definition:Measurable set
Let $X$ be a measurable space. The measurable sets in $X$ are the members of the $\sigma$-algebra.
:::

::: definition:Borel set
Let $X$ be a topological space. The Borel sets in $X$ are the members of the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the topology.
:::

::: definition:F-sigma set
The $F_\sigma$ sets are the members of the countable unions of all closed sets.
:::

::: definition:G-delta set
The $G_\delta$ sets are the members of the countable intersections of all open sets.
:::

#### 3. Function

::: definition:Continuous function
Let $X$ and $Y$ be topological spaces and $f : X \to Y$. $f$ is continuous if $f^{-1}(V)$ is open in $X$ for every open $V$ in $Y$.
:::

::: definition:Locally continuous function
Let $X$ and $Y$ be topological spaces, $f : X \to Y$ and $x_0 \in X$. $f$ is locally continuous at $x_0$ if for every open $V$ containing $f(x_0)$ in $Y$, there is an open $W$ containing $x_0$ in $X$ s.t. $W \subset f^{-1}(V)$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ and $Y$ be topological spaces and $f : X \to Y$. $f$ is continuous iff $f$ is locally continuous at every point of $X$.
:::

::: definition:Measurable function
Let $X$ be a measurable space with $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{M}$, $Y$ a topological space and $f : X \to Y$. $f$ is measurable if $f^{-1}(V)$ is measurable in $X$ for every open $V$ in $Y$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a measurable space with $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{M}$, $Y$ a topological space and $f : X \to Y$. Then $\Omega$, consisting of all sets $E \subset Y$ s.t. $f^{-1}(E) \in \mathcal{M}$, is a $\sigma$-algebra in $Y$.
:::

换言之，满射函数构建一个新的 sigma 代数。

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a measurable space, $Y$ a topological space and $f : X \to Y$ measurable. If $E$ is a Borel set, then $f^{-1}(E) \in \mathcal{M}$.
:::

换言之，满射可测函数构建的 sigma 代数包含 Borel set。

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a measurable space and $f : X \to [-\infty, \infty]$ an extended real function. If $f^{-1}((\alpha, \infty]) \in \mathcal{M}$ for every $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$, then $f$ is measurable.
:::

告诉我们证明 measurable 的方式是证明开集是否可以由已知在 $\mathcal{M}$ 中的集合构建出来。

::: definition:Borel measurable function
Let $X$ and $Y$ be topological spaces and $f : X \to Y$. $f$ is Borel measurable if $f^{-1}(V)$ is a Borel set for every open $V$ in $Y$.
:::

::: proposition
Every continuous function is Borel measurable.
:::

#### 4. Composite Function

::: proposition
Let $X$, $Y$, $Z$ be topological spaces, $f : X \to Y$ and $g : Y \to Z$. If $f$ and $g$ are continuous, then $h = g \circ f$ is continuous.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a measurable space, $Y$, $Z$ topological spaces, $f : X \to Y$ and $g : Y \to Z$. If $f$ is measurable and $g$ is continuous, then $h = g \circ f$ is measurable.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a measurable space, $Y$, $Z$ topological spaces, $f : X \to Y$ and $g : Y \to Z$. If $f$ is measurable and $g$ is Borel measurable, then $h = g \circ f$ is measurable.
:::

::: proposition
Let $u, v : X \to \mathbb{R}$ be real measurable functions. Then $f = \max\{u, v\}$ and $g = \min\{u, v\}$ are measurable, as are $f^-$ and $f^+$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $u, v : X \to \mathbb{R}$ be real measurable functions. Then $f = u + iv$ is a complex measurable function.
:::

::: proposition
Let $f = u + iv$ be a complex measurable function. Then $u$, $v$ and $|f|$ are real measurable functions.
:::

::: proposition
Let $f$ and $g$ be real or complex measurable functions. Then $f + g$ and $f \cdot g$ are measurable functions.
:::

::: proposition
Let $f$ be a complex measurable function. Then $f = \alpha \cdot |f|$ for some complex measurable function $\alpha$.
:::

#### 5. Function Sequence

::: proposition
Let $\{f_n\}$ be a sequence of non-negative measurable functions (NMF). Then $\underset{n \geq 1}{\sup}\, f_n$ and $\underset{n \to \infty}{\limsup}\, f_n$ are measurable.
:::

::: proposition
The pointwise limit of a convergent sequence of real or complex measurable functions is measurable.
:::

::: proposition
Let $f$ be a NMF. Then there exists an increasing sequence of non-negative simple measurable functions whose pointwise limit is $f$.
:::

#### 6. Integration on Positive Function

::: definition:Integral of a positive function
Let $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ be a measure space and $E \in \mathcal{M}$. For a non-negative simple measurable function $s$ with value $\alpha_i$ on $A_i$,
$$\int_E s \, d\mu = \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i \mu(A_i \cap E).$$
For a NMF $f$,
$$\int_E f \, d\mu = \underset{0 \le s \le f}{\sup} \int_E s \, d\mu,$$
where the supremum is over all simple measurable $s$ with $0 \le s \le f$.
:::

::: note
For NMFs $f, g$, a constant $c \ge 0$ and $E \in \mathcal{M}$:
- $\int_E (f + g) \, d\mu = \int_E f \, d\mu + \int_E g \, d\mu$
- $\int_E c f \, d\mu = c \int_E f \, d\mu$
:::

::: proposition
Let $\{f_n\}$ be an increasing sequence of NMFs with $f_n \to f$ pointwise. Then $\int_X f_n \, d\mu \to \int_X f \, d\mu$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $\{f_n\}$ be a sequence of NMFs and $f = \sum_{n=1}^\infty f_n$ pointwise. Then $\int_X f \, d\mu = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \int_X f_n \, d\mu$.
:::

对于正函数，函数收敛积分和函数级数积分没有任何条件。

::: proposition
Let $\{f_n\}$ be a sequence of NMFs. Then $\int_X \underset{n \to \infty}{\liminf}\, f_n \, d\mu \leq \underset{n \to \infty}{\liminf} \int_X f_n \, d\mu$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $f$ be a NMF and $\phi(E) = \int_E f \, d\mu$ for $E \in \mathcal{M}$. Then $\int_X g \, d\phi = \int_X g \cdot f \, d\mu$ for every NMF $g$.
:::

#### 7. Integration on Complex Function

::: definition:L^1 space
Let $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ be a measure space. $L^1(\mu)$ is the collection of all complex measurable functions $f$ on $X$ s.t. $\int_X |f| \, d\mu < \infty$.
:::

::: note
For $f = u + iv \in L^1(\mu)$,
$$\int_X f \, d\mu = \int_X u^+ \, d\mu + \int_X u^- \, d\mu + \int_X v^+ \, d\mu + \int_X v^- \, d\mu.$$
For $f, g \in L^1(\mu)$, complex $\alpha, \beta$ and $E \in \mathcal{M}$:
- $\int_E (\alpha f + \beta g) \, d\mu = \alpha \int_E f \, d\mu + \beta \int_E g \, d\mu$
- $\left| \int_X f \, d\mu \right| \leq \int_X |f| \, d\mu$, with equality when $\alpha f = |f|$
:::

::: proposition
Let $\{f_n\}$ be complex measurable functions with $|f_n| \leq g$ for some $g \in L^1(\mu)$ and $f_n \to f$ pointwise. Then $f \in L^1(\mu)$, $\underset{n \to \infty}{\lim} \int_X f_n \, d\mu = \int_X f \, d\mu$ and $\underset{n \to \infty}{\lim} \int_X |f_n - f| \, d\mu = 0$.
:::

条件是每个 $f_n$ 的绝对值都小于等于 $g$（被控制）。

::: proposition
Let $\{f_n\}$ be complex measurable functions with $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \int_X |f_n| \, d\mu < \infty$. Then $\sum_{n=1}^\infty f_n$ converges pointwise to some $f$, $f \in L^1(\mu)$ and $\int_X f \, d\mu = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \int_X f_n \, d\mu$.
:::

条件是 $f_n$ 绝对值的积分之和有限，蕴含 $f_n \in L^1(\mu)$。条件也可以是 $\int_X \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} |f_n| \, d\mu < \infty$。

::: proposition
Let $f$ be a NMF and $E \in \mathcal{M}$. If $\int_E f \, d\mu = 0$, then $f = 0$ a.e. on $E$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $f \in L^1(\mu)$. If $\int_E f \, d\mu = 0$ for every $E \in \mathcal{M}$, then $f = 0$ a.e. on $X$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $\mu(X) < \infty$ and $f \in L^1(\mu)$. Set $A_E(f) = \frac{\int_E f \, d\mu}{\mu(E)}$. If $A_E(f)$ lies in a closed set $S$ in the complex plane for every $E \in \mathcal{M}$ with $\mu(E) > 0$, then $f(x) \in S$ for almost all $x \in X$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $\{E_k\}$ be a sequence of measurable sets in $X$ with $\sum_{k=1}^\infty \mu(E_k) < \infty$. Then almost all $x \in X$ lie in at most finitely many of the sets $E_k$.
:::

## Chapter 2

#### 1. Space

::: definition:Vector space
Let $V$ be a set equipped with addition and scalar multiplication. $V$ is a vector space if for all $x, y, z \in V$ and scalars $a, b$:
- $x + y \in V$
- $x + y = y + x$
- $(x + y) + z = x + (y + z)$
- $x + 0 = x$
- $x + (-x) = 0$
- $a \cdot x \in V$
- $a(x + y) = a x + a y$
- $(a + b)x = a x + b x$
- $a(b x) = (a b)x$
- $1 x = x$
:::

::: definition:Hausdorff space
Let $X$ be a topological space. $X$ is a Hausdorff space if for any $p, q \in X$ with $p \neq q$, $p$ has a neighborhood $U$ and $q$ has a neighborhood $V$ s.t. $U \cup V = \emptyset$.
:::

::: note
- Every metric space is a Hausdorff space.
- $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a locally compact Hausdorff space.
:::

#### 2. Set

::: definition:Closed set
Let $X$ be a topological space and $E \subset X$. $E$ is closed if its complement is open.
:::

::: definition:Closure
Let $X$ be a topological space and $E \subset X$. The closure $\overline{E}$ is the smallest closed set in $X$ which contains $E$.
:::

::: definition:Compact set
Let $X$ be a topological space and $K \subset X$. $K$ is compact if every open cover of $K$ contains a finite subcover.
:::

::: definition:Locally compact space
Let $X$ be a topological space. $X$ is locally compact if every point of $X$ has a neighborhood whose closure is compact.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a topological space, $K \subset X$ compact and $F \subset X$ closed. If $F \subset K$, then $F$ is compact.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a topological space and $A \subset B \subset X$. If $B$ has a compact closure, then $A$ has a compact closure.
:::

::: proposition
In a Hausdorff space, every compact set is closed.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a Hausdorff space, $K \subset X$ compact and $F \subset X$ closed. Then $F \cap K$ is compact.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a Hausdorff space, $K \subset X$ compact and $p \in K^c$. Then there are open sets $U$ and $W$ s.t. $p \in U$, $K \subset W$ and $U \cap W = \emptyset$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a Hausdorff space and $\{K_a\}$ a collection of compact sets. If $\bigcap_a K_a = \emptyset$, then there is a finite subcollection of $\{K_a\}$ whose intersection is $\emptyset$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space, $U \subset X$ open and $K \subset X$ compact. If $K \subset U$, then there is an open set $V$ s.t. $\overline{V}$ is compact and $K \subset V \subset \overline{V} \subset U$.
:::

::: definition:Sigma-compact set
A $\sigma$-compact set is a countable union of compact sets.
:::

::: definition:Sigma-finite measure
A measure is $\sigma$-finite if the space is a countable union of sets of finite measure.
:::

#### 3. Function

::: proposition
Let $X$ and $Y$ be topological spaces and $K \subset X$ compact. If $f : X \to Y$ is continuous, then $f(K)$ is compact.
:::

::: definition:Linear functional
Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces. A linear functional $\wedge$ maps $V$ to $W$ s.t. $\wedge(\alpha x + \beta y) = \alpha \wedge x + \beta \wedge y$.
:::

::: definition:Lower semicontinuous function
Let $X$ be a topological space and $f : X \to [-\infty, \infty]$. $f$ is lower semicontinuous if $\{x : f(x) > \alpha\}$ is open for every real $\alpha$.
:::

::: note
- The characteristic function of an open set is lower semicontinuous.
- The supremum of a collection of lower semicontinuous functions is lower semicontinuous.
:::

::: definition:Upper semicontinuous function
Let $X$ be a topological space and $f : X \to [-\infty, \infty]$. $f$ is upper semicontinuous if $\{x : f(x) < \alpha\}$ is open for every real $\alpha$.
:::

::: note
- The characteristic function of a closed set is upper semicontinuous.
- The infimum of a collection of upper semicontinuous functions is upper semicontinuous.
:::

::: proposition
A function is continuous iff it is both lower semicontinuous and upper semicontinuous.
:::

::: definition:Support
Let $X$ be a topological space and $f$ a complex function on $X$. The support of $f$ is the closure of the set $\{x : f(x) \neq 0\}$.
:::

::: definition:C_c(X)
Let $X$ be a topological space. $C_c(X)$ is the collection of all continuous complex functions on $X$ whose support is compact.
:::

::: note
- $C_c(X)$ is a vector space.
- The range of $f \in C_c(X)$ is compact.
:::

::: definition:K ≺ f
Let $X$ be a topological space, $K \subset X$ compact and $f \in C_c(X)$. $K \prec f$ if $0 \leq f \leq 1$ and $f(x) = 1$ for $x \in K$.
:::

::: definition:f ≺ V
Let $X$ be a topological space, $V \subset X$ open and $f \in C_c(X)$. $f \prec V$ if $0 \leq f \leq 1$ and $f(x) = 0$ for $x \in V^c$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space, $V \subset X$ open and $K \subset X$ compact. If $K \subset V$, then there exists $f \in C_c(X)$ s.t. $K \prec f \prec V$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space, $V_1, \ldots, V_n \subset X$ open and $K \subset X$ compact. If $K \subset V_1 \cup \cdots \cup V_n$, then there exist functions $h_i \prec V_i$ s.t. $h_1(x) + \cdots + h_n(x) = 1$ for $x \in K$.
:::

有 $K \prec h_1 + \cdots + h_n \prec V_1 \cup \cdots \cup V_n$。若 $K \prec f \prec V$，则 $f = h_1 f + \cdots + h_n f$ 且 $h_i f \prec V_i$。

#### 4. Measure

::: theorem:Riesz representation theorem
Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space and $\wedge$ a positive linear functional on $C_c(X)$. Then there exists a $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{M}$ containing all Borel sets and a unique positive measure $\mu$ s.t.:
1. $\wedge f = \int_X f \, d\mu$ for $f \in C_c(X)$.
2. $\mu(K) < \infty$ for compact $K$.
3. $\mu(E) = \inf\{\mu(V) : E \subset V,\ V \text{ open}\}$ for $E \in \mathcal{M}$.
4. $\mu(E) = \sup\{\mu(K) : E \subset K,\ K \text{ compact}\}$ for open $V$ and $E \in \mathcal{M}$ with $\mu(E) < \infty$.
5. If $E \in \mathcal{M}$, $\mu(E) = 0$ and $A \subset E$, then $A \in \mathcal{M}$.
:::

::: definition:Inner regular measure
A measure $\mu$ is inner regular if $\mu(E) = \sup\{\mu(K) : E \subset K,\ K \text{ compact}\}$ for $E \in \mathcal{M}$.
:::

::: definition:Outer regular measure
A measure $\mu$ is outer regular if $\mu(E) = \inf\{\mu(V) : E \subset V,\ V \text{ open}\}$ for $E \in \mathcal{M}$.
:::

::: definition:Regular measure
A measure is regular if it is both inner regular and outer regular.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a locally compact, $\sigma$-compact Hausdorff space. Then the measure in the Riesz representation theorem is regular. Moreover, if $E \in \mathcal{M}$ and $\epsilon > 0$, there is a closed set $F$ and an open set $V$ s.t. $F \subset E \subset V$ and $\mu(V - F) < \epsilon$; and if $E \in \mathcal{M}$, there is an $F_\sigma$ set $A$ and a $G_\delta$ set $B$ s.t. $A \subset E \subset B$ and $\mu(B - A) = 0$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space in which every open set is $\sigma$-compact, and $\lambda$ a positive Borel measure with $\lambda(K) < \infty$ for every compact $K$. Then $\lambda$ is regular.
:::

::: proposition
Every set of positive measure has nonmeasurable subsets.
:::

::: theorem:Lebesgue measure
On $\mathbb{R}^k$ there is a positive complete regular measure $m$ defined on a $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{M}$ s.t.:
1. $m(W) = \text{vol}(W)$ for every $k$-cell $W$.
2. $\mathcal{M}$ contains all Borel sets, and $E \in \mathcal{M}$ iff there is an $F_\sigma$ set $A$ and a $G_\delta$ set $B$ s.t. $A \subset E \subset B$ and $\mu(B - A) = 0$.
3. $m(E + x) = m(E)$ for $E \in \mathcal{M}$ and $x \in \mathbb{R}^k$.
4. If $\mu$ is a positive translation-invariant Borel measure on $\mathbb{R}^k$ with $\mu(K) < \infty$ for every compact $K$, then there is a constant $c$ s.t. $\mu = c m$.
5. For a linear transformation $T : \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^k$, $m(T(E)) = |\det(T)| \, m(E)$ for $E \in \mathcal{M}$.
:::

#### 5. Measurable Functions

::: theorem:Lusin's theorem
Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space and $f$ a complex measurable function. Assume there is a set $A$ s.t. $f(x) = 0$ for $x \notin A$, $\mu(A) < \infty$, and let $\epsilon > 0$. Then there exists $g \in C_c(X)$ s.t. $\mu(\{x : f(x) \neq g(x)\}) < \epsilon$ and $\underset{x \in X}{\sup} |g(x)| \leq \underset{x \in X}{\sup} |f(x)|$.
:::

::: note
If $|f| \leq 1$, then there is a sequence $\{g_n\}$ s.t. $g_n \in C_c(X)$, $|g_n| \leq 1$ and $f(x) = \underset{n \to \infty}{\lim}\, g_n(x)$ a.e.
:::

::: theorem:Vitali-Caratheodory theorem
Let $f \in L^1(\mu)$ be a real function and $\epsilon > 0$. Then there exist functions $u$ and $v$ s.t. $u \leq f \leq v$, $u$ is upper semicontinuous and bounded above, $v$ is lower semicontinuous and bounded below, and $\int_X (v - u) \, d\mu < \epsilon$.
:::

## Chapter 3

#### 1. Convex Function

::: definition:Convex function
Let $\phi$ be a real function defined on $(a, b)$, where $-\infty \leq a < b \leq \infty$. $\phi$ is convex if $\phi((1 - \lambda)x + \lambda y) \leq (1 - \lambda)\phi(x) + \lambda \phi(y)$ whenever $a < x < b$, $a < y < b$ and $0 \leq \lambda \leq 1$; equivalently, $\frac{\phi(t) - \phi(s)}{t - s} \leq \frac{\phi(u) - \phi(t)}{u - t}$ whenever $a < s < t < u < b$.
:::

::: note
A convex function is continuous on the open segment.
:::

::: theorem:Jensen's inequality
Let $\mu$ be a positive measure, $\Omega \in \mathcal{M}$ with $\mu(\Omega) = 1$, and $f \in L^1(\mu)$ a real function. If $a < f(x) < b$ for $x \in \Omega$ and $\phi$ is convex on $(a, b)$, then $\phi\left(\int_\Omega f \, d\mu\right) \leq \int_\Omega \phi \circ f \, d\mu$.
:::

::: note
Consequences of Jensen's inequality:
- $(x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n)^{1/n} \leq \frac{1}{n}(x_1 + x_2 + \cdots + x_n)$ for $x_i > 0$.
- $x_1^{a_1} x_2^{a_2} \cdots x_n^{a_n} \leq a_1 x_1 + a_2 x_2 + \cdots + a_n x_n$ where $\sum a_i = 1$.
- $e^{\int_\Omega \log g \, d\mu} \leq \int_\Omega g \, d\mu$ for $\mu(\Omega) = 1$.
- $\int_X f g \, d\mu \leq \left(\int_X f^p \, d\mu\right)^{1/p} \left(\int_X g^q \, d\mu\right)^{1/q}$ where $f, g$ are NMFs, $1 < p < \infty$ and $\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1$; equality holds when $\alpha f^p = \beta g^q$ a.e. with $\alpha, \beta$ not both $0$.
- $\left(\int_X (f + g)^p \, d\mu\right)^{1/p} \leq \left(\int_X f^p \, d\mu\right)^{1/p} + \left(\int_X g^p \, d\mu\right)^{1/p}$ where $f, g$ are NMFs and $1 < p < \infty$; equality holds when $f = \lambda g$ a.e. with $\lambda > 0$.
:::

#### 2. $L^p$ Space

::: definition:Essential supremum
Let $f$ be a NMF. The essential supremum of $f$ is $\inf\{\alpha : \mu(f^{-1}((\alpha, \infty])) = 0\}$.
:::

::: definition:C_0(X)
Let $X$ be a topological space. $C_0(X)$ is the class of all continuous functions on $X$ s.t. $\underset{x \to \infty}{\lim} f(x) = 0$.
:::

::: definition:L^p-norm
Let $0 < p < \infty$. The $L^p$-norm of $f$ is $\|f\|_p = \left(\int_X |f|^p \, d\mu\right)^{1/p}$, and $\|f\|_\infty$ is the essential supremum of $f$.
:::

::: definition:L^p space
Let $0 < p \leq \infty$. $L^p(\mu)$ is the set of measurable functions $f$ with $\|f\|_p < \infty$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $1 \leq p \leq \infty$, $\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1$, $f \in L^p(\mu)$ and $g \in L^q(\mu)$. Then $fg \in L^1(\mu)$ and $\|fg\|_1 \leq \|f\|_p \|g\|_q$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $1 \leq p \leq \infty$, $f \in L^p(\mu)$ and $g \in L^p(\mu)$. Then $f + g \in L^p(\mu)$ and $\|f + g\|_p \leq \|f\|_p + \|g\|_p$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $1 \leq p \leq \infty$, $f \in L^p(\mu)$ and $\alpha$ a complex number. Then $\alpha f \in L^p(\mu)$ and $\|\alpha f\|_p = |\alpha| \|f\|_p$.
:::

::: proposition
Considering functions equal a.e. as equivalent, for $1 \leq p \leq \infty$ and a positive measure $\mu$, $L^p(\mu)$ is a complete metric space.
:::

::: proposition
Let $S$ be the class of all complex measurable simple functions $s$ with $\mu(\{x : s(x) \neq 0\}) < \infty$. If $1 \leq p < \infty$, then $S$ is dense in $L^p(\mu)$.
:::

::: proposition
If $1 \leq p < \infty$, then $C_c(X)$ is dense in $L^p(\mu)$.
:::

::: proposition
In a locally compact Hausdorff space, $C_0(X)$ is the completion of $C_c(X)$ relative to the metric defined by $\|f\|_\infty$.
:::

## Chapter 4

### 1. Space

::: definition:Inner product space
Let $V$ be a complex vector space with a form $(\cdot, \cdot)$. $V$ is an inner product space if for all $x, y, z \in V$ and scalars $\alpha$:
- $(x, y) = \overline{(y, x)}$
- $(x + y, z) = (x, z) + (y, z)$ and $(x, y + z) = (x, y) + (x, z)$
- $(\alpha x, y) = \alpha(x, y)$ and $(x, \alpha y) = \overline{\alpha}(x, y)$
- $(x, x) \geq 0$
- $(x, x) = 0$ iff $x = 0$
:::

::: note
An inner product space is a complex vector space, a topological space, a measurable space, a normed space, a metric space and a Hausdorff space.
:::

::: theorem:Schwarz inequality
Let $x, y$ be elements of an inner product space. Then $|(x, y)| \leq \|x\| \|y\|$.
:::

::: theorem:Triangle inequality
Let $x, y$ be elements of an inner product space. Then $\|x + y\| \leq \|x\| + \|y\|$.
:::

::: definition:Hilbert space
A Hilbert space is a complete inner product space.
:::

::: note
- $\mathbb{C}^n$ is a Hilbert space.
- $L^2(\mu)$ is a Hilbert space with the inner product $\int_X f \overline{g} \, d\mu$.
:::

::: proposition
In a Hilbert space, the inner product and the norm are continuous.
:::

::: proposition
Let $H$ be a Hilbert space and $L$ a continuous linear functional on $H$. Then there is a unique $y \in H$ s.t. $Lx = (x, y)$ for $x \in H$.
:::

::: definition:l^2 space
The space $l^2$ is $L^2(\mu)$ where $\mu$ is the counting measure and $X$ is a countable set.
:::

::: definition:Partially ordered set
A set with a relation $\leq$ is partially ordered if:
- $a \leq b$ and $b \leq c$ imply $a \leq c$
- $a \leq a$
- $a \leq b$ and $b \leq a$ imply $a = b$
:::

::: proposition
Every nonempty partially ordered set contains a maximal totally ordered subset.

### 2. Set

::: definition:Subspace
Let $V$ be a vector space and $M \subset V$. $M$ is a subspace of $V$ if $M$ is a vector space; that is, $x + y \in M$ and $\alpha x \in M$ for all $x, y \in M$ and scalars $\alpha$.
:::

::: definition:Convex set
Let $V$ be a vector space and $E \subset V$. $E$ is convex if $(1 - t)x + t y \in E$ for all $x, y \in E$ and $0 < t < 1$.
:::

::: proposition
In a Hilbert space, a closed convex set $E$ has a unique element of smallest norm.
:::

::: definition:x-perp
Let $x$ be an element of an inner product space. Then $x^\perp$ is the set of vectors $y$ s.t. $(x, y) = 0$.
:::

::: definition:M-perp
Let $M$ be a subset of an inner product space. Then $M^\perp$ is the set of vectors $y$ s.t. $(x, y) = 0$ for all $x \in M$.
:::

::: proposition
$M^\perp$ is closed.
:::

::: proposition
Let $H$ be a Hilbert space and $M$ a closed subspace. Then every $x \in H$ can be written $x = Px + Qx$ with $Px \in M$ and $Qx \in M^\perp$, where $Px$ is the closest point in $M$ to $x$ and $Qx$ is the closest point in $M^\perp$ to $x$. The mappings $P$ and $Q$ are linear, and $\|x\|^2 = \|Px\|^2 + \|Qx\|^2$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $H$ be a Hilbert space and $M$ a closed subspace. If $M \neq H$, then there exists a vector $y \neq 0$ s.t. $y \in M^\perp$.
:::

### 3. Orthonormal Set

::: definition:Orthonormal set
A set $\{u_\alpha\}$ in an inner product space is orthonormal if $(u_\alpha, u_\beta) = 0$ for $\alpha \neq \beta$ and $\|u_\alpha\| = 1$.
:::

::: definition:Maximal orthonormal set
An orthonormal set $\{u_\alpha\}$ is maximal if no further vector can be adjoined to it while keeping the set orthonormal.
:::

::: proposition
Let $X$ and $Y$ be metric spaces with $X$ complete, and $f : X \to Y$ continuous. If $X_0$ is a dense subset of $X$, $f$ is an isometry on $X_0$, and $f(X_0)$ is dense in $Y$, then $f$ is an isometry of $X$ onto $Y$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $H$ be a Hilbert space, $\{u_\alpha : \alpha \in A\}$ an orthonormal set, and $P$ the space of all finite linear combinations of the vectors $u_\alpha$. Then $\sum_{\alpha \in A} |\hat{x}(\alpha)|^2 \leq \|x\|^2$ for every $x \in H$. Moreover, restricted to $\overline{P}$, the map $x \to \hat{x}$ is continuous and is an isometry of $\overline{P}$ onto $l^2(A)$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $H$ be a Hilbert space and $\{u_\alpha\}$ an orthonormal set. The following are equivalent:
- $\{u_\alpha\}$ is a maximal orthonormal set in $H$.
- The set $P$ of all finite linear combinations of members of $\{u_\alpha\}$ is dense in $H$.
- $\sum_{\alpha \in A} |\hat{x}(\alpha)|^2 = \|x\|^2$ for $x \in H$.
- $\sum_{\alpha \in A} \hat{x}(\alpha) \overline{\hat{y}(\alpha)} = (x, y)$ for $x \in H$ and $y \in H$.
:::

::: definition:Isomorphism
An isomorphism is a linear mapping $\wedge : H_1 \to H_2$ s.t. $(\wedge x, \wedge y) = (x, y)$ for $x, y \in H_1$.
:::

::: proposition
In a Hilbert space $H$, every orthonormal set $B$ is contained in a maximal orthonormal set.
:::

### 4. Trigonometric Series

::: definition:L^p(T)
$L^p(T)$ is $L^p(\mu)$ whose domain is $[-\pi, \pi]$.
:::

::: definition:Trigonometric polynomial
A trigonometric polynomial is a function $f(t) = \sum_{n=-N}^{N} c_n e^{int}$.
:::

::: proposition
Let $f \in C(T)$ and $\epsilon > 0$. Then there is a trigonometric polynomial $P$ s.t. $\|f - P\|_\infty < \epsilon$.
:::

这证明三角多项式在 $L^2$ 空间中是稠密的，说明 $\{e^{int}\}$ 是一个完备基，从而说明傅立叶展开在 $L^2$ 空间中是收敛的。

::: definition:Fourier coefficient
The Fourier coefficient of $f$ is $\hat{f}(n) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(t) e^{-int} \, dt$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
:::

::: definition:Fourier series
The Fourier series of $f$ is $\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty} \hat{f}(n) e^{int}$.
:::

::: definition:Fourier partial sum
The Fourier partial sum of $f$ is $\sum_{-N}^{N} \hat{f}(n) e^{int}$.
:::
